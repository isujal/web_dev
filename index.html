What is FRC, in one breath.

Every January, FIRST reveals a brand-new game to thousands of high-school teams on the same day. From then, students have about 8 weeks to design, build and program a robot of up to ~125 lb from scratch. At events, robots play in alliances of 3 v 3, teaming up with strangers in one match and facing them in the next, across 2 min 30 s matches: a short autonomous period where the code drives alone, then the drivers take over.

It is the closest thing school students get to a real engineering company: deadlines, budgets, sponsors, a pit crew and a crowd. FIRST calls the spirit of it Gracious Professionalism: compete hard, help your rivals anyway.

Seven divisions. One robot.

Students run the team. Each division owns part of the robot or the organisation, and every member picks a main division plus extras.

The regiment is recruiting.

No robotics experience needed. If you are a CAJCS student who likes building, coding, designing, filming or organising, there is a division for you.

Who can join?

CAJCS students. Our mentors confirm the grades for each intake; tell us yours in the form and we will tell you if this intake fits.

How much time does it take?

Regular sessions in the Maker Lab through the year, with more during build season (January to March). Exact session times are shared with members.

Do I need to know how to code or build?

No. Basic training uses our 154-task skills library, starting at the Minimum level. You learn by doing, with mentors beside you.

For parents: is it safe?

Work happens in the school Maker Lab under mentor supervision, with safety glasses and training before any power tool. We never publish a student's name or photo without your written consent.
